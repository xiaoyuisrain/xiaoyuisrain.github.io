I am a computational linguist working at the intersection of natural language processing (NLP), linguistics, and cognitive science.
I received my PhD in NLP from the University of Amsterdam,
under the supervision of Dr [Ekaterina Shutova](https://www.shutova.org/) and Dr [Martha Lewis](https://marthaflinderslewis.github.io/).
My thesis investigated the capabilities of unimodal and multimodal large language models (LLMs) for the processing of incongruity, as manifested in metaphor and humour.
I am currently continuing my research as an independent researcher,
and I am open to new job opportunities.

My research has evolved from asking how well AI systems understand deeper or complex meaning (meaning that is non-literal, indirect, multimodal, culturally situated, or open to interpretation)
to asking what it means for such systems to be used in real-world social contexts.
I develop theory-informed datasets, annotation frameworks, benchmarks, and evaluation protocols for unimodal and multimodal LLMs.
My work combines computational evaluation with cross-cultural behavioural studies, statistical modelling, and qualitative analysis to compare model behaviour with human standards or baselines.
Through projects on metaphor, humour, communicative intention, and cultural alignment, I study not only what models can do, but also whose meanings, cultural assumptions, and communicative norms they represent or overlook.

My broader goal is to contribute to **human-centred and socially responsible AI** -- AI that is evaluated in relation to the people, languages, cultures, and institutions in which it operates.
I am particularly interested in the responsible application of AI in high-impact communicative settings,
including education and healthcare.
One of my ongoing projects is concerned with AI-assisted speech-language therapy for people with aphasia.
